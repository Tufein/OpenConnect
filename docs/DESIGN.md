# The OpenConnect look

A fresh install opens in the **editorial** style: a calm page with Instrument Serif display type, IBM Plex Sans running
text, very large rounded cards, hairline borders and a floating tab bar with smooth line icons. The default colours are
**Olive**, warm cream with deep olive in light mode and warm charcoal-green with soft sage in dark mode. The sporty
**Sleek** style and **Graphite** are still here, as are the original **Handheld** console look (v0.31.0) as *Retro* and
the formal *Classic* style. The colour choice is deliberately small — Olive, Sand, Forest, Graphite, High contrast and
the phone's Wallpaper — and an old saved key that is no theme any more opens as Olive. Everything below lives in
`android/app/src/main/kotlin/nl/tufein/openconnect/ui/`.

The editorial page opens with a full-width landscape photograph under a warm scrim (`EditorialHero`), five metric
orbs, a calm ring card for the chosen front-page metric, serif insight cards for readiness and sleep, and a journal
check-in. The card under the battery and the battery, journal and *Last 7 days* cards are chosen in Settings. The
photograph is by Luca Bravo on Unsplash (Unsplash License); fonts and credits are listed in `CREDITS.md`.

## The rules

| | Sleek (`clean`) | Retro (`retro`) |
|---|---|---|
| **Type** | *IBM Plex Sans* for headlines, numbers and sentences, compact and semi-bold, with tighter tracking on big titles; *IBM Plex Mono* for labels, units and data. | *Jersey 10* (a pixel face made for sports shirts) for headlines and big numbers, Plex Sans for sentences, Plex Mono for labels. |
| **Corners** | Restrained corners of 8, 10, 12, 16 and 20 dp (`ModernShapes`): enough to read as a panel, not a bubble. | Corners cut in pixel steps (`PixelCornerShape`): two steps of half the corner size, like a rounded corner on graph paper. |
| **Panels** | `Panel` in `Pixel.kt`: a flat surface with a 1 dp hairline border, no shadow and 14 dp padding when the caller uses the default. A tint is only a 10% wash. | Flat surface, 2 dp ink outline and a hard offset shadow (3 dp right and down, no blur). |
| **Colour** | Graphite: cool neutral surfaces (`#0E1116` dark, `#F4F6F8` light) with one blue accent (`#4C9AFF` / `#1D4ED8`). Each body signal keeps its own colour in every theme. | The same palettes. |
| **Icons** | Our own small grid icons (`PixelIcons.kt`), written as text and drawn by `PixelIcon`; no icon pack. The editorial tab bar and quiet controls use the smooth `LineIcons.kt` set. | The pixel grid icons throughout. |
| **Logo** | A rounded heart with a pulse through it, drawn as a vector (`res/drawable/ic_launcher_*.xml`, generated by `android/tools/launcher_icons.py`); eight smooth variants, each with a themed one-colour version. | The pixel heart (`PixelIcons.Logo`). |
| **Controls** | `PrimaryButton`, `SecondaryButton` and `QuietButton` are compact rectangles with a 10 dp radius and a hairline outline; a chosen `PixelChip` is filled with the accent colour. `PixelSwitch` has a round knob, `PixelProgress` still uses 24 blocks for progress. | A pressed button sinks onto its hard shadow and the chosen chip is filled with ink, like a highlighted menu entry. |
| **Charts** | `SegmentBar` draws one rounded bar and `RingGauge` one smooth arc with rounded ends. Date axes and chart content descriptions are unchanged. | Block bars, a square-capped line and a dial of 40 ticks. |
| **Tab bar** | A flat strip with a hairline above it and a short accent line under the open tab (`ModernBar` in `Navigation.kt`); every tab is at least 56 dp high. | A flat strip under an ink line, the open tab a block filled with ink. |
| **Hierarchy** | Score tiles show numbers only; the 7-day lines sit in a folded *Trends* section, and the month chart and recent activities fold away with their headline value in the header (`FoldSection`). Health keeps the scores on top and draws **one chart at a time**, chosen by tabs (`health_chart`). Insights keeps six everyday tabs and puts the other six behind *More*. Folds and tabs are for secondary information only. | The same structure, with the console look. |

No gradients and no blur are used, except for the scrim over the editorial photo; emphasis comes from colour, a tint or
a shadow. A `tint` washes a little of a signal colour into a panel.

## Colour themes, letters and the launcher icon

**Colour themes** (*Settings, Appearance*) come in four groups, seven in all, each with a dark and a light version:

- **Calm**: Olive (the default; warm cream and deep olive in light mode, warm charcoal-green and soft sage in dark
  mode) and Sand (the same warmth with clay and straw). Forest goes a shade deeper green.
- **Neutral**: Graphite (cool neutrals with one blue accent) and High contrast (pure black and white).
- **Retro screens** (v1.15.0): LCD green, four shades of green like an old handheld screen (our own tints, not copied
  from any console). Dark is the unlit screen with light-green pixels, light the pale green glass with dark pixels.
- **Phone**: Wallpaper, the phone's own colours on Android 12 and newer.

A key from an older version that is no theme any more opens as Olive, so an old setting never crashes and never brings
back a look that is no longer offered.

**The retro pack** (v1.15.0, all off until chosen, all our own design): in the Retro style a switch draws **scanlines**
over the whole app (`Modifier.scanlines()` in `Retro.kt`: one 1 dp line of 10 % black every 3 dp, so text keeps its
contrast). The content always sits in the same `Box`, lines or not, so switching them never rebuilds the screen. In every
style a switch plays a short **8-bit tune** when a sync brings something new while the app is on screen
(`core/retro/Chiptune.kt` computes a 25 % pulse wave with soft note edges; `AudioTrackChiptunePlayer` plays it as a
system sound). No image or sound file is added to the APK. Since v1.16.0 a third switch, **level-up messages**,
announces a trophy or a level that new data unlocks: `core/retro/LevelUp.kt` compares the trophies before and after a
refresh, names a single trophy, counts several, and stays quiet on the first count after opening and on a jump of more
than three trophies or two levels (an import, the demo). With the sounds on, a trophy plays the record tune and a level
the goal tune. The message is a normal snackbar, so TalkBack reads it and there is no animation to switch off. Since v1.17.0 the
Trophies tab has a **hall of fame** (`HiScoreCard.kt`): per distance the five fastest runs from
`BestEfforts.highScores` (each run once), places in cabinet style ("1ST" to "5TH", the same in every language; TalkBack
reads each row as a sentence instead) and three initials (`core/retro/Initials`, A to Z only, AAA until chosen) set on a
name entry with an arrow up and down per letter. It is not called "Hi-score" because the year card already has that name.

Shapes and layout are the same in all of them. A theme is a pair of `Keys` in `PaletteKeys` (`Theme.kt`): primary,
secondary, tertiary, background, panel and text colour, and from them `scheme()` builds the whole Material colour scheme.
`ThemesLettersAndIconTest.everyThemeIsReadableInDarkAndInLight` holds every theme to the same limits: text on the
background and on a panel at least 7:1, small grey text at least 4.5:1, text on each accent colour at least 4.5:1 and
the main colour against a panel at least 3:1 (WCAG contrast ratios). A new theme is one more entry in `Palette`, one in
`PaletteKeys` and a name in both string files; the test tells you if it is too faint.

A theme saved by an older version (Arcade, Clean, Midnight) no longer exists and opens as Handheld. Ocean and Sunset
exist again, with new colours.

**Letters** (*Settings, Appearance, Letters*) only apply to Retro: *Pixel letters* is the console look above, *Clear
letters* swaps Jersey and Plex Mono for Plex Sans. Editorial, Sleek and Classic always use the clear type.

**Launcher icon** (*Settings, App icon*): eight icons, each an `activity-alias` of the main screen in the manifest
(`.IconHandheld`, `.IconNight`, and so on) with its own adaptive icon in `res/mipmap-anydpi-v26`. Exactly one alias is
switched on at a time; `AppIcons.select` turns the new one on before the old ones off, and `AppIcons.current` reads which
is on from the system, so it also survives clearing the app's data. The picture of each icon is made from the same pixel
grids as the app by `android/tools/launcher_icons.py` (the colours in that script and in `AppIcon.kt` must match; the
first icon is hand-made). The launcher may take a moment to show the new icon, and on some phones the app closes once.

The home-screen widget and the short start-up background follow the chosen palette too. `HomeWidgetViews` paints its
`RemoteViews` from `colorSchemeFor` (it cannot read the Compose theme), and `HomeWidget.refresh` is called when a palette,
style or dark mode is picked. The window background in `values/colors.xml` and `values-night/colors.xml` uses the two
Graphite background colours so a start-up in another theme does not flash cream. The editorial hero photograph is
decoded from `res/drawable-nodpi/hero_editorial.jpg`.

Since v0.43.0 the whole look is one section of Settings (*Appearance*) that is folded away until its header is tapped
(`FoldSection` in `Components.kt`): dark or light, style, letters (Retro only), colour themes and the launcher icon. The
header shows what is chosen now (for example "Olive · Editorial"). At the top sits a live **preview** (`LookPreview`):
a small dashboard drawn in the chosen theme, so a colour or style can be judged before it is kept instead of from two
little squares. The first start shows the same preview with a choice of style and theme (`OnboardingScreen`).

Since v1.9.1 the same section also carries three app-wide choices below the style and colour rows: **signal colours**
(soft theme tints or a clear colour per signal), **ring weight** (thin 2 dp or bold 4.5 dp) and the **front-page
photo** light (following the hour or fixed). They are stored under `theme_signals`, `theme_rings` and `theme_hero`, read
through `Look`, and handed to the tree as `LocalRingWeight` and `LocalHeroChoice`, so the orbs, tiles, charts and both
editorial heroes follow without each screen passing a parameter.

The watch faces (Clean, Dashboard, Retro, see [WATCH_FACES.md](WATCH_FACES.md)) are not part of the app since v0.43.0: they are Connect IQ code
for the watch and keep their own three designs.

## Adding a screen

1. Put content in `SurfaceCard` or `Panel`, titles in `SectionTitle`, numbers in `displayMedium` or `titleLarge` and
   sentences in `bodyMedium`. Take text styles from the theme: they change with the style and the phone's font scale.
2. Use the controls above, never `Button`, `FilterChip`, `Switch` or an icon from the Material icon set.
3. A new icon is a new grid in `PixelIcons.kt`: `#` is filled, anything else is empty, and every row has the same length.
4. Never add a gradient or a blur, except for the editorial photo scrim. If something needs emphasis, use a tint, a
   colour or a weight. Check the screen in editorial, Sleek and Retro, at 200% font size and in dark and light mode.
5. Fold only secondary information, with its headline value in the fold header (`FoldSection`). The day's key numbers and
   anything a test or the daily glance needs stay visible.

## Styles

`Style` in `Theme.kt` is saved as `theme_style` and read through `LocalStyle`:

- **Editorial** (`editorial`, the default): `EditorialShapes` (12 to 36 dp), Instrument Serif display type, hairline
  borders, pill buttons and the photo hero.
- **Sleek** (`clean`): the sporty dashboard, with `ModernShapes`, compact panels, rectangular controls and clear letters.
- **Retro** (`retro`): the original Handheld console look, with pixel-cut corners, ink outlines and a hard shadow.
- **Classic** (`classic`): small corners (2 to 8 dp), hairlines, no shadow, clear letters with serif titles.

Shapes come from `MaterialTheme.shapes`, so everything that uses them follows the style. Outlines use `lineWidth()` and
`inkColor()`, the hard shadow uses `shadowColor()` (transparent outside Retro) and `softShadow()` lifts modern cards; use
those rather than fixed values. Only Retro has a choice of letters. What does not change with the style: the launcher
icon. Every colour theme works in every style.
