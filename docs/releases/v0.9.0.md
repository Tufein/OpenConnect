## OpenConnect v0.9.0: weekly goals, training load and a roadmap

### Install

Download `openconnect-v0.9.0.apk` below and open it. It installs **over v0.8.0 and keeps your data and your keys**
(same signing key). Needs Android 8.0 or newer. Check the download against `openconnect-v0.9.0.apk.sha256`.

### What is new

- **Weekly goals.** *Settings, Weekly goals*: intensity minutes (default 150), distance (20 km) and activities (3), with
  plus and minus buttons; a goal at 0 is off. *Home* shows *Goals this week* with a bar of 20 segments per goal, the real
  amount (4 of 3 reads as 4) and how many goals you reached.
- **Training load.** A card on Home compares this week with your average of the last four weeks, with six weekly bars,
  the dashed line being your usual week, and a verdict: *Light week*, *Steady*, *Heavy week* or *Big jump*, each with a
  sentence on what it means. It needs two weeks of activities. It is OpenConnect's own estimate (minutes times an effort
  weight from your heart rate), not Garmin's Training Load, and not medical advice.
- **A roadmap.** `docs/ROADMAP.md` lists what is shipped and what comes next, and what needs something only the owner can
  supply (a real fenix 8 test, live AI key checks).

### What changed in the code

- `core/Goals.kt` (`WeeklyGoals`, `GoalProgress`) and `core/TrainingLoad.kt` (`TrainingLoadCalculator`, `LoadStatus`):
  pure Kotlin, 11 tests including the thresholds. The effort weight goes by average heart rate as a share of the highest
  heart rate you have recorded: under 60% counts 1, then 1.5, 2, 3 and from 90% 4; without heart rate an activity counts 2.
  The verdict compares the last 7 days with a quarter of the last 28: below 0.8 light, up to 1.3 steady, up to 1.5 heavy,
  above that a big jump.
- `SettingsStore.weeklyGoals` keeps the goals; `HomeState` carries the goal progress and the load; `HomeScreen.kt` has
  `GoalsCard` and `LoadCard`; `SettingsScreen.kt` has the steppers. English and Dutch.
- Version 0.9.0, versionCode 9.

### Known limits

- The load verdict is a rule of thumb from your own history; with little history it shows nothing rather than guess.
- The Bluetooth sync is still untested on a real watch.
